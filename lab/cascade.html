Information cascades

Ten language-model players guess which urn was chosen. Each sees one private ball plus everyone's earlier guesses. Do they herd, and into wrong answers? A replication of Anderson & Holt (1997) with small in-browser models. Naming game →

How this works
  1. A coin picks urn A or urn B. One urn holds 2 red balls and 1 blue; the other 2 blue and 1 red. Which colour belongs to which urn is swapped between sequences so the model can't lean on a fixed association.
  2. Players go one at a time. Each privately draws a ball (it points to the right urn 2 times in 3) and sees all earlier guesses, but not earlier balls.
  3. The rational benchmark: follow your own ball until the earlier guesses lead by 2, then follow the lead whatever you drew. That is a cascade. It is individually rational, and it wrongly locks in about 1 sequence in 5.
